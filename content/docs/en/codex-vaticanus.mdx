# Codex Vaticanus

Codex Vaticanus is the Vatican Library's Greek Bible of the fourth century: in the Library's words, "the oldest almost
complete Bible in Greek". It is kept as manuscript Vat. gr. 1209, which is why scholars also call it B.

## Date and history

The codex is dated to the fourth century, and by some to its first half. It is first found in the Vatican Library in
the fifteenth century: it entered the collections in the period of Popes Nicholas V and Sixtus IV, when the Library
received some of its most important manuscripts, and the old catalogues record it there.

## What it lacks

Most of the codex survives, but not all of it. It has lost:

- the beginning of Genesis, up to 46:28;
- part of the Psalms, from Psalm 105:27 to 137:6 in the Greek numbering (106:27 to 138:6 in the Hebrew);
- the end of the Letter to the Hebrews, from 9:14;
- the letters to Timothy and Titus, the Letter to Philemon, and Revelation.

## Why it matters

With [Codex Sinaiticus](/docs/codex-sinaiticus) and [Codex Alexandrinus](/docs/codex-alexandrinus) it is one of the
three manuscripts on which Rahlfs' standard edition of the [Septuagint](/docs/septuagint) mainly rests, and one of the
chief witnesses of the New Testament: it does not have the longer ending of Mark (see [the New Testament's
text](/docs/new-testament-text)).

## Seeing it

The Vatican Library has digitized the whole manuscript: it can be read page by page on
[DigiVatLib](https://digi.vatlib.it/view/MSS_Vat.gr.1209). The Library has also published scholarly studies of it, among
them *Il Codex Vaticanus a Bisanzio* (Studi e Testi 556, 2022).
