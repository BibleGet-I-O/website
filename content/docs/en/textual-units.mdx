# Textual units and CBTUDR

"Tobit 5:4" is not the same text in every Bible. Chapters begin at different places (Genesis 31:55 in the Vulgate is
Genesis 32:1 in the Hebrew numbering); one edition's verse is two in another (the Clementine Vulgate's Tobit 5:3–4 is CEI
2008's Tobit 5:4); some editions print verses out of order; whole books follow different recensions. A reference names
a place in one edition's numbering, not a piece of text. [Why editions differ](/docs/why-editions-differ) shows what this
looks like in the reader; this page describes the project's answer for developers and students: the **Common Biblical
Textual Unit Data Repository** (CBTUDR), curated by the Catholic Engineering Task Force of the Catholic Digital Commons
Foundation.

## Textual units

CBTUDR gives each **textual unit** a stable identifier, `btu:`, independent of any edition's numbering. For each
edition it then publishes which units each verse contains. With the two, an application can line up two editions verse
by verse, even across chapter boundaries; carry a reference from one edition to another; and define a passage, such as a
lectionary reading, once for every edition.

The registry holds identifiers and verse structure only, **never Bible text**. Mapping a copyrighted edition records how
it is numbered, not what it says.

## The Nova Vulgata as reference

The units are seeded from the verses of the **Nova Vulgata**, the Church's Latin Bible ([the Vulgate](/docs/vulgate)),
which the instruction *Liturgiam authenticam* (2001) makes "the point of reference as regards the delineation of the
canonical text" for translations used in the liturgy. Each of its verses, as published on vatican.va, is a unit to
begin with: 35,776 units for the 73 books. A unit is then **split** where an edition divides it, and text the Nova
Vulgata lacks gets a unit of its own.

Some features of the Nova Vulgata carry into the units. Its Psalms are numbered the Hebrew way, with the superscription
counted as verse 1 ([the Psalms' two numberings](/docs/why-editions-differ#psalm-numbering)). Esther is one book, the
Hebrew text with the Greek additions as lettered verses (`btu:est.1.1a`). Where the Nova Vulgata prints a verse number in
brackets, without text, because the critical text omits it (33 such numbers, Matthew 17:21 among them), the unit exists
and is marked `nv_omitted`, so that the editions that print text there can map to it.

## The identifiers

```text
btu:tb.5.4          the unit that began as Nova Vulgata Tobit 5:4
btu:tb.5.4.1        its first part, once some edition divides it
btu:tb.5.4.1.2      the second part of that part
btu:est.1.1a        a lettered verse of the Nova Vulgata itself (Esther's Greek additions)
btu:tb.5.4+1        text the Nova Vulgata lacks, placed after Tobit 5:4
```

The book segment is the Holy See's Latin abbreviation, lowercased (`tb`, `1sam`, `eccli`, `apc`). Splits are numbered,
not lettered, so that a split can never be mistaken for one of the Nova Vulgata's own lettered verses. A parent unit is
exactly its children, in order: a link to `btu:tb.5.4` stays valid when the unit is split, so nothing that already
links to it needs to change.

An identifier records where the unit was first created, for readability. **It is not a reference** and must not be
parsed as one: `btu:tb.5.4` is "the unit that began as Nova Vulgata Tobit 5:4", not "Tobit 5:4 in your edition".
Translating between numberings is the job of the edition maps.

Under the CDCF URI scheme, the full identifier is `cdcf:btu:tb.5.4`. The scheme's `cdcf:verse/…` identifies a
*reference*, a citation read in some numbering; a `btu:` identifier names the *text*, and an edition's map connects the
two.

## Edition maps

For each edition, the registry lists, in the edition's own reading order, the units each of its verses contains:

```json
{"edition": "vgcl", "book": "tb", "verses": [
  {"ref": "5:3", "units": ["btu:tb.5.4.1"], "kind": "corresponding", "status": "reviewed"},
  {"ref": "5:4", "units": ["btu:tb.5.4.2"], "kind": "corresponding", "status": "reviewed"}
]}
```

- **`ref`** is the edition's own verse: `10:18a`, or `1:1a@GREEK` where an edition prints both texts of Esther.
- **`kind`** is `same` (the same text, renumbered or re-divided) or `corresponding` (a different recension or source
  text: the passage corresponds, but it is not the same text, as with the Vulgate's longer Tobit).
- **`status`** is `rule` (made by a documented rule, such as a chapter boundary or the Psalms), `draft` (proposed by a
  tool, not yet reviewed) or `reviewed`.

All seven editions on this site are mapped. About nine links in ten are made by rule; the rest are drafts proposed by
tools, where no rule decides (where the text itself differs, as in Tobit, Job, Sirach, Greek Esther and Exodus 40, by
comparing the verses' meaning with a multilingual sentence-embedding model, LaBSE), and they are being reviewed one by
one. The registry's validator checks that
every verse covers whole units, and that no unit is covered twice by the same text.

## In the reader

Today the reader's comparison still lines verses up by their numbers, and the Psalms by their Hebrew reference. Lining
editions up by unit, so that the Clementine Vulgate's Tobit 5:3–4 stands beside CEI 2008's Tobit 5:4, is the next step,
once the maps are reviewed and released.
