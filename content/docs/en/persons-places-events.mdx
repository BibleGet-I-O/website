# Persons, places and events and CBPPEDR

Many people and places in the Bible share a name. There are four Philips in the New Testament; Libnah is both a city of
Judah and a camp of Israel in the desert. An index of names, or a link from a name to what it means, has to know which
one each passage means. The **Common Biblical Persons, Places and Events Data Repository** (CBPPEDR), curated by the
Catholic Engineering Task Force of the Catholic Digital Commons Foundation, gives each person, place and event of the
Bible a stable identifier, and says where each appears.

Its identifiers are drafts pending the committee's review until its release v1.0.0.

## The identifiers

```text
bpers:philippus.herodiadis-maritus   Philip, son of Herod the Great, first husband of Herodias (Mt 14:3)
bpers:philippus.tetrarcha            Philip, son of Herod the Great, tetrarch of Ituraea (Lk 3:1)
bplace:lobna.urbs                    Libnah, a city of the Shephelah
bplace:lebna.castra                  Libnah, a camp of Israel in the desert
bevent:exilium-babylonicum           the Babylonian exile
```

An identifier is `bpers:`, `bplace:` or `bevent:` followed by the entry's Latin name as the Nova Vulgata spells it, in
lower case, with a short Latin word after a dot where the name is shared. The three types have separate catalogs, so an
application can build a separate index of each.

## What an entry holds

- its **names** in Latin (the Nova Vulgata's form), Italian and English;
- a short **description** of the repository's own, in Italian and English, written to tell the entry apart from its
  homonyms;
- the **textual units** where it appears, as identified by [CBTUDR](/docs/textual-units), so that the same entry serves
  every Bible edition: a verse is listed where the text names the entry, or designates it by a title the context ties to
  it, not where it is only meant by a pronoun;
- **labels**, for a verse where the entry is called otherwise (Cephas for Peter, "the king of Israel" for Joram): how each
  edition names it there, since an edition may name or designate the entry where another does not;
- its **relations** to other entries: parent, spouse, child; the place of a people; part of a region;
- its **Wikidata** item, where one has been settled;
- the **evidence** for all of the above, cited by location.

The repository holds no text of the works it cites, and of the Bible editions only the few words of a label (at most
six).

## Better unidentified than misidentified

Each statement is recorded at the level of certainty its evidence allows, and no higher. A verse is attested, or two
entries are the same person, only when the Bible text itself shows it, or an authoritative source states it as fact.
What the text leaves open but scholars propose (that Thaddaeus is Judas son of James, that the beloved disciple is John)
is recorded as a **conjecture**, with each opinion for and against it and whose it is. A conjecture never changes what
the catalog states: there are 189, of which 96 are supported, 26 disputed, 25 doubted and 11 rejected by authoritative
sources, while 31 rest only on leads. Where a verse could mean more than one bearer of a name, it is given to one only
where a source states which and none doubts it; otherwise it is recorded as a conjecture, and a bearer the sources leave
unidentified gets an entry of its own.

## Sources

The catalog is built from two printed works as evidence: the *Nuovo Dizionario Enciclopedico Illustrato della Bibbia*
(Piemme 2005) and the notes and appendices of *La Bibbia di Gerusalemme* (EDB 2011). Both are under copyright: the
repository takes facts from them (names, identities, relations, verse references), never their text, and cites them by
headword or note and page. Catholic biblical scholarship and peer-reviewed studies are the authorities for conjectures;
other works are recorded as leads only. STEPBible's TIPNR list was used only to find names the two works miss, each then
checked against the Nova Vulgata's text.

## Wikidata

An entry's Wikidata item is matched in one of three ways: by a strict automatic rule (a candidate of the class "human
biblical figure", or the only fitting candidate whose description is explicitly biblical); by a model that compares the
entry's names, relatives and verses with each candidate's facts, accepted only when it judges one candidate the same with
probability 0.8 or more and every other below 0.2, and then checked by hand; or by a reviewer. Of 4,305 entries, 778 are
matched by the rule, 644 by the model and 12 by a reviewer; the rest are left for review. The automatic matches have not
been reviewed yet, and some prove to be another bearer of the name, so this site links only the items set by the model
or a reviewer.

## The indices in the reader

Each edition's ⚙ menu in the reader offers its indices of persons, places and events. They are made from CBPPEDR: an
entry's textual units become that edition's verses through CBTUDR's map of the edition, using only the links made by rule
or reviewed. A verse where the entry is called by another name or a title is listed only for the editions whose text
was checked to name it there. An entry the repository marks for review is shown as uncertain.
