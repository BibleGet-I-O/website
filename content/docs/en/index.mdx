# Documentation

These pages tell how the text of the Bible reached us and why its editions differ. Before the history of a text, it
helps to recall what the Church believes this book to be. The Second Vatican Council set it out in the dogmatic
constitution [*Dei Verbum*](https://www.vatican.va/archive/hist_councils/ii_vatican_council/documents/vat-ii_const_19651118_dei-verbum_en.html)
(1965), on divine revelation. Its teaching, in brief, follows; the numbers are those of its articles.

## God who reveals himself

God "chose to reveal Himself and to make known to us the hidden purpose of His will", by "deeds and words having an
inner unity": his deeds in the history of salvation confirm what the words teach, and the words proclaim the deeds and
make their mystery clear (2). He prepared this revelation through the patriarchs, Moses and the prophets (3), and brought
it to completion in Jesus Christ, "who is both the mediator and the fullness of all revelation" (2, 4). After him "we now
await no further new public revelation" before his glorious return (4).

## Revelation and faith

The answer to God who reveals is "the obedience of faith", "by which man commits his whole self freely to God" (5). Faith
is itself a gift: the grace of God and the help of the Holy Spirit move the heart to believe, and the same Spirit
constantly deepens the understanding of what is believed (5).

## Scripture and Tradition

Christ entrusted the Gospel to the Apostles. They handed it on by their preaching, their example and their observances,
and they and "apostolic men", under the inspiration of the Holy Spirit, committed the message of salvation to writing.
To keep the Gospel "forever whole and alive within the Church", they left bishops as their successors (7). This
Tradition "develops in the Church with the help of the Holy Spirit", as believers grow in understanding of what was handed
on; and through it "the Church's full canon of the sacred books is known" (8).

Sacred Tradition and Sacred Scripture, "flowing from the same divine wellspring, in a certain way merge into a unity and
tend toward the same end". Scripture is the word of God "consigned to writing under the inspiration of the divine
Spirit"; Tradition hands on the word of God entrusted to the Apostles in its full purity. So "it is not from Sacred
Scripture alone that the Church draws her certainty about everything which has been revealed", and both are to be
received "with the same sense of loyalty and reverence" (9). Together they "form one sacred deposit of the word of God,
committed to the Church". Its authentic interpretation is entrusted to the Church's living teaching office, which "is not
above the word of God, but serves it". Tradition, Scripture and the teaching office are so joined "that one cannot stand
without the others" (10).

## Inspiration and truth

The books of both Testaments, "in their entirety, with all their parts", are sacred and canonical because, written under
the inspiration of the Holy Spirit, "they have God as their author". God chose men who, using their own powers and
abilities, wrote as "true authors". So the books teach "solidly, faithfully and without error that truth which God wanted
put into sacred writings for the sake of salvation" (11).

## Reading Scripture

Because "God speaks in Sacred Scripture through men in human fashion", the reader looks for what the sacred writers
really meant, with attention to "literary forms" (history, prophecy, poetry and others) and to the ways of speaking and
narrating of their time and culture. Scripture is also to be read "in the sacred spirit in which it was written": with
attention to "the content and unity of the whole of Scripture", to "the living tradition of the whole Church", and to the
harmony of the truths of faith (12). The words of God, expressed in human language, "have been made like human
discourse, just as the word of the eternal Father, when He took to Himself the flesh of human weakness, was in every way
made like men" (13).

## The Old and the New Testament

The books of the Old Testament, written under divine inspiration, "remain permanently valuable" (14). They prepared for
the coming of Christ, and in them "the mystery of our salvation is present in a hidden way" (15). In the words of Saint
Augustine, God arranged "that the New Testament be hidden in the Old and the Old be made manifest in the New" (16).
Among all the Scriptures the Gospels have "a special preeminence", as "the principal witness for the life and teaching of
the incarnate Word" (18). The Church asserts their historical character: they "faithfully hand on what Jesus Christ, while
living among men, really did and taught" (19).

## Scripture in the life of the Church

The Church "has always venerated the divine Scriptures just as she venerates the body of the Lord", and offers the
faithful "the bread of life from the table both of God's word and of Christ's body"; together with Tradition she holds
them as "the supreme rule of faith" (21). "Easy access to Sacred Scripture should be provided for all the Christian
faithful": this is why the Church received as her own the Greek translation of the Old Testament called the
[Septuagint](/docs/septuagint), gives a place of honour to the Latin [Vulgate](/docs/vulgate), and sees to it "that
suitable and correct translations are made into different languages, especially from the original texts of the sacred
books" (22). The Council urges all the faithful to read Scripture often, for, as Saint Jerome wrote, "ignorance of the
Scriptures is ignorance of Christ"; and it asks that prayer accompany the reading, "so that God and man may talk
together" (25).

## These pages

The pages below follow the text of Scripture from the Hebrew books and their Greek translation, through the great
manuscripts and Jerome's Latin, to the editions you can read on this site, and tell why those editions do not always
agree: a verse has another number, a book is longer, a psalm is counted differently. The reader's small "why?" marks open
[why editions differ](/docs/why-editions-differ). The last part describes the project itself: its
[textual units](/docs/textual-units), its [data and API](/docs/data-and-api), and [how to contribute](/docs/contributing).
Pages on each edition on this site will follow.
